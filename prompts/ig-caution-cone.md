# PERMANENT WHEN POSTED — IG CAROUSEL COVER
## PRODUCTION SPECIFICATION · STILL PHOTOGRAPH
**4:5 VERTICAL | 1080 × 1350 DELIVERY | GENERATE AT 2160 × 2700**

Companion plate to `ig-caution-cone.prompt.txt`. Second cover in the safety-
object series, after `ig-cash-paid-in-cash.md`.

---

## 0 · WHY THIS IMAGE EXISTS

The first cover treats an untraceable cash purchase as a crime scene: yellow
tape, PAID IN CASH, DO NOT CROSS. This one treats an edit to a posted ledger
entry as a slip hazard.

The series grammar is now fixed: **a yellow safety object, on wet ground,
applied with total sincerity to a bookkeeping problem.** Nothing in the
photograph may acknowledge the joke. The tape worked because the plate was
straight — cinematic, wet, unlit by any wink. The moment the world becomes
comedic the image stops being an ad and becomes a meme.

Indoors this time, because a caution sign is a building object and a ledger
lives in an office. The floor stays wet so the ground rhymes with the
forecourt: same photographer, same hour, different room.

**THE FLOOR IS HALF THE FRAME, AND IT IS THERE FOR TYPE.** Cover one put the
tape at 44% and let wet concrete fill everything below it, so the copy could be
set onto a real surface carrying real reflections. That is what stops a post
looking designed. This plate repeats the structure: sign in the upper half, wet
wood filling the lower, type laid into it.

**TWO COLOURS, NOT FIVE.** Safety yellow against warm dark hardwood, and
nothing else of consequence. A grey office is colourless and dies at thumbnail
size; yellow on wet brown survives it. The slide grounds for this post get
sampled from the plate's own shadows, the way #171511 was sampled from the
forecourt.

---

## 1 · SUBJECT

A standard yellow folding A-frame caution sign — the hinged two-panel type used
for wet floors — standing alone on a wet dark hardwood floor at night, a few
feet in front of a closed wooden office door.

Its printed face carries a slip pictogram and the words **PERMANENT WHEN
POSTED**, with smaller text beneath reading **reversing entry required**.

The sign is the only sharp object in the photograph.

---

## 2 · FRAME

**Camera height:** 0.55 m, tilted 4° DOWN. Low enough that the sign reads as
consequential, tilted enough that the floor takes the lower half of the frame.
Level or tilted up, the floor disappears and there is nowhere for the type.

**Distance:** 1.8 m. Focus plane locked on the printed face, which puts the
boards immediately around the base inside the plane too — that is the detail
the eye actually lands on.

**Camera angle:** roughly 15° off the sign's face, so it reads as a solid
three-dimensional object with one panel receding, not as a flat graphic.

**Lens:** 85 mm prime, 11-blade rounded aperture.

**Aperture:** f/2.0. The printed face is sharp; the desks behind dissolve.

**Shutter:** 1/125 s. **ISO:** 400. **Sensor:** full-frame, 45 MP class.

---

## 3 · LIGHT

**Overhead office fluorescents, mostly off.** One bank still lit further back,
4000 K, throwing long reflections down the wet floor. This is the after-hours
look — the hour an accountant is actually still working.

**Warm desk lamp**, out of frame to the right, 2700 K, raking across the sign's
face so the plastic's texture and the stencilled edges read.

**Cool ambient** from a window, 6500 K, filling shadows so nothing crushes.

**Ratio:** roughly 3:1 warm practical to cool ambient — the same split that
structures the forecourt plate.

The printed face stays evenly lit with no hotspot. Nothing in frame is a
visible light source.

---

## 4 · THE CONE

Injection-moulded polypropylene, safety yellow, visibly used rather than new.

- Hinged A-frame, both panels open, standing square on the floor
- Scuffs and grey scratch marks along the bottom edges
- A shallow crack at one corner where it has been dropped
- Faint dried mop-water streaks across the lower half
- Slight sheen on the moulded surface, matte in the recesses
- Moulded ribbing running vertically on the panel edges
- A little dust caught in the texture

---

## 5 · THE PRINTED FACE

Read top to bottom. This is the part the whole image exists for, so it must be
crisp, level and correctly spelled.

**Pictogram, upper half.** The standard wet-floor slip figure: a person falling
backwards, both arms thrown up, one leg kicked forward, drawn as a solid black
silhouette with short motion arcs behind. **One deviation only — loose paper
receipts fly out of the raised hand**, three or four small rectangles tumbling
away with the motion. Everything else about the pose is the ordinary sign.

**Main text, lower half**, black stencilled capitals, condensed grotesque, two
lines, centred:

```
PERMANENT
WHEN POSTED
```

**Fine print** beneath it, much smaller, same black, lower case:

```
reversing entry required
```

**Character:** flat screen-printed ink sitting in the plastic, very slightly
worn at the letter edges, one or two characters marginally lighter where the
ink has thinned with age. Not pristine vinyl.

---

## 6 · THE GROUND — the most detailed thing in the frame

Dark stained hardwood, board floor, wet from a mop that passed minutes ago.
This is in the focus plane and it carries the type, so it gets the detail
budget the background gives up.

**The boards**
- Narrow planks, 90-120 mm, running away from camera toward the door
- Visible open grain with the darker late-wood lines raised very slightly
- Butt joints staggered, thin dark gaps between boards holding water
- Two or three boards noticeably lighter where the stain has worn through
- A shallow scuff arc where a chair or door has swung across repeatedly
- One small dent with the finish broken, pale wood showing

**The water**
- A thin film, never puddles, following the grain direction
- Curving mop arcs still visible as slightly wetter bands
- Dry patches lifting on the high points, breaking the reflections up
- Water sitting darker in the board gaps, tracing every joint
- The film is what makes the light streak; the dry patches interrupt it

**The reflection**
- The sign's yellow smeared downward into the boards beneath it, broken by grain
- A warm vertical bar of door-light stretched toward camera
- Reflections soften and lose colour with distance from the sign

---

## 7 · THE AIR

Still, indoor, late. A very slight haze from the overhead bank, no atmospheric
effects. Nothing dramatic — this room is ordinary.

---

## 8 · BACKGROUND — the door, specified for tone, rendered soft

A closed wooden office door, roughly 2 m behind the sign, filling most of the
upper background. It is described precisely below so the bokeh carries the
right colour and mass — **not so it renders sharp.** At f/2.0 at this distance
it should be thoroughly out of focus. Detail belongs on the floor.

- Panelled hardwood, dark stained, warmer and redder than the floor
- Four recessed panels, readable only as soft rectangular tonal steps
- A brass lever handle, tarnished, catching one small highlight
- A warm strip of light escaping underneath, pooling onto the wet boards
- Worn paint or scuffing at the base where feet have kicked it

Nothing else. No desks, no monitors, no filing cabinets, no signage. The door
is a warm wall of tone whose only job is to make the yellow sit forward.

**Nothing behind the sign may be legible.** The only readable thing in the
frame is the sign itself.

---

## 9 · OPTICS & SENSOR

- **Bokeh:** circular, going slightly cat's-eye toward the corners
- **Longitudinal CA:** faint green fringing behind the focus plane, magenta in
  front, only at high-contrast edges
- **Vignetting:** 0.6 stop falloff at the corners, uncorrected
- **Highlight rolloff:** soft shoulder on the fluorescent bank, no hard clipping
- **Grain:** fine luminance grain, ISO 400 character
- **Micro-contrast:** high on the stencilled ink, falling away with distance

## 12 · REFERENCE IMAGE

Use `ref-forecourt-dusk.png` — the tape-free crop of the first cover's lower
forecourt — with this wording:

> Match the light quality, colour temperature, wet reflective ground, film
> grain and depth-of-field character of the reference. Do not reproduce its
> composition or its location.

The reference exists to carry the *optics* across the series, not the place.
This plate is indoors; only the way the light behaves on wet ground should
survive. Feeding the full first cover instead would hand back barrier tape,
which is the one object that must not appear here.

---

## 13 · TYPE — ADDED IN POST

None on the plate itself. The sign carries all its own lettering.

Slide type is set separately in Creato Display over the upper-left and
lower-right clear zones. `ref-forecourt-dusk.png` measured 12.3:1 for white in
its lower right; measure this plate before deciding whether it needs a scrim.

**Fallback if the lettering comes back wrong:** regenerate with the printed face
blank and composite the pictogram and text in code. The pictogram is arcs and a
silhouette, and the type is Creato — both are drawable. Only fall back if
several generations fail, since a rendered sign will always beat a composited
one for believability.

---

## 14 · REJECT

- Any comedy in the photograph itself: tilted framing, cartoon lighting, wacky
  props. The sign is the joke; the world is straight
- Misspellings, uneven baselines, or letters that drift off the panel
- A traffic cone instead of a folding A-frame sign
- A pristine, unused, showroom-clean sign
- Barrier tape anywhere in frame
- People, faces, hands, readable documents, logos, brand names
- Legible text anywhere except the sign
- Deep depth of field
- A hotspot or specular blowout across the printed face
- Daylight, or a bright fully-lit office. After hours only
- Puddles rather than a thin mop film
- HDR look, crushed blacks, clipped fluorescents
